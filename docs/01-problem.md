# Problem

Food businesses, dining centers, farms, stores, and event organizers can have usable surplus food with only a short window before it becomes unusable. Nearby community organizations may need that food but still need to determine whether they can accept it, store it, receive it in time, and handle the quantity.

## Primary users

- Donors: restaurants, farms, dining centers, grocery stores, event organizers
- Recipients: food pantries, shelters, nonprofits, community organizations
- Future users: volunteer drivers and coordinators

## Core pain points

1. Finding a suitable recipient quickly
2. Confirming accepted food categories
3. Matching storage requirements
4. Matching available capacity
5. Coordinating before the pickup deadline
6. Explaining why a destination was selected
7. Keeping a traceable status record

## Product hypothesis

Smaller and highly perishable donations can be rescued more effectively when matching considers urgency, operational capacity, storage, local need, and pickup feasibility together.
